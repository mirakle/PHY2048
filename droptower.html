Kinematic equations

Ideal free fall

v(t) = v₀ − g·t

y(t) = h + v₀·t − ½·g·t²

v² = v₀² + 2g(h − y)

Up is positive, so gravity's acceleration is −g — constant, so the speed chart is a straight line between impacts.

With quadratic drag

dv/dt = −g − (g / vt²)·v·|v|

v(t) → −vt while falling

solved numerically (RK4)

Drag always opposes motion — it slows the fall as speed nears −v_t, and it also slows any upward rebound.

Each time the ball reaches the ground it bounces: v → −e·v, where e is the coefficient of restitution above. e = 1 keeps all the kinetic energy (bounces forever, idealized); e = 0 loses it all on first contact.
